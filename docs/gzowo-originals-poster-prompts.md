# Gzowo Originals — poster prompts

Three directions for turning a frame from your own footage into a poster. Feed
the same still into each, compare, pick one. Whichever wins becomes the fixed
prompt, so every Original ends up looking like it came from the same place.

**How to use.** In ChatGPT, attach the frame and paste one prompt. Replace
`{TITLE}` with the film's title. Leave everything else alone — the constraints
are what make separate posters look like a set rather than three unrelated
pictures.

**Pick the frame carefully.** One clear subject, plenty of empty space around it,
nothing important near the bottom third — the title goes there.

---

## A · Cinematic, cold and wide

> Turn this photograph into a film poster. Keep the original subject and
> composition recognisable — do not replace or reimagine the scene. Grade it to a
> cold, desaturated palette: deep blue-greys in the shadows, a single warm
> low-sun accent on the subject. Deepen the blacks so the frame sits in darkness,
> add fine 35mm grain and a subtle anamorphic light streak. Leave the lower third
> almost empty. Place the title `{TITLE}` there in a condensed uppercase sans
> serif, thin weight, wide letter-spacing, plain white, small relative to the
> frame. No other text, no credits block, no logos, no border. Portrait 2:3.

Reads like a serious science-fiction release. Best fit for rocket launches, night
sky, anything with a hard silhouette. Pairs naturally with the black-and-white
interface.

---

## B · Polish poster school

> Reinterpret this photograph as a Polish-school film poster. Do not aim for
> photographic realism — translate the subject into hand-painted shapes with
> visible brush texture and slightly imperfect edges. Restrict the palette to
> three inks: off-white paper, deep black, and a single saturated accent colour
> drawn from the photograph. Allow one surreal liberty in the composition. Place
> the title `{TITLE}` in hand-lettered uppercase, irregular baseline, integrated
> into the artwork rather than laid over it. No other text, no logos. Portrait 2:3.

The most characterful and the most local. Fair warning: it fights the interface —
every poster becomes an object competing for attention, and the rule that colour
comes only from the artwork will make the whole screen swing hard on each one.

---

## C · Natural history

> Turn this photograph into a nature documentary poster. Keep the scene intact and
> photographic. Grade it towards warm late-afternoon light: golden highlights,
> soft green and amber midtones, gentle haze receding into the distance. Widen the
> sense of landscape and keep the horizon calm. Place the title `{TITLE}` in the
> lower third, in a light-weight serif with generous letter-spacing, warm white,
> quietly authoritative. No other text, no logos, no border. Portrait 2:3.

Suits Gzowo as a place — fields, weather, seasons, the plot itself. Less suited to
rockets, which want the cold treatment in A.

---

## Recommendation

Start with **A**. It matches the interface, it flatters the kind of footage you
actually shoot, and it is the only one of the three that will still look right
sitting in a row next to Hollywood posters — which is exactly where it will sit.

Keep **C** in reserve for a possible second row of purely place-based material.
